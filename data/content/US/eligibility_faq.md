# United States: who can apply, in brief

**This is not legal advice.** It repeats what official publications say in general terms. It
cannot tell you whether you qualify. For your own situation, read the official eligibility page
(<https://www.uscis.gov/naturalization-eligibility>, the address the study guide gives) or talk to
a licensed immigration attorney or an accredited representative.

## What does a person need in order to naturalize?

The official study guide lists these requirements: [1]

- be 18 years old;
- be a lawful permanent resident;
- submit an application for naturalization with fees;
- meet the requirements for residence and presence in the United States;
- be a person of good moral character and show attachment to the principles and ideals of the
  Constitution;
- be able to read, write, speak and understand English, and have knowledge and an understanding
  of U.S. history and government (civics);
- complete the naturalization test and interview;
- attend a naturalization ceremony and take the Oath of Allegiance.

## How many years of residence are needed?

The sources gathered for this pack do not give a number. The study guide says only that an
applicant must "meet the residence and presence in the U.S. requirements". [1] Check the official
eligibility page.

## What level of English is needed?

The requirement is to read, write, speak and understand English. [1] At the interview this is
tested by speaking with the officer, reading aloud 1 of 3 sentences, and writing 1 of 3
sentences. [2] No level on a language scale is given in the sources gathered.

## Is anyone excused from the civics test, or given an easier one?

- **Age 65 or older with 20 years as a lawful permanent resident:** a shorter list of 20
  questions, in the language of your choice, with 10 asked and 6 needed. [3]
- **Disability:** the civics requirement does not apply to a person who cannot demonstrate the
  knowledge because of a medically determinable physical or mental impairment lasting, or
  expected to last, at least 12 months. [4]

## How much does it cost?

The study guide says the application is submitted "with fees" but gives no amount. [1] The fee is
not in the sources gathered.

## Are there other ways to become a citizen?

Yes. Besides naturalization, the study guide names being born in the United States under the
conditions set by the 14th Amendment, and deriving citizenship under conditions set by
Congress. [1]
