# United States: the day of the civics test

This is general information drawn from the official pages listed at the end. It is not legal
advice. Rules change: check the official pages, or your appointment notice, before your interview.

## Which test you take

- If your Form N-400 was filed **on or after 20 October 2025**, you take the 2025 civics test. [1]
- If it was filed before that date, you take the 2008 test: up to 10 questions from a list of 100,
  with 6 correct to pass. [2]

## Format of the 2025 civics test

- It is an **oral** test. A USCIS officer asks the questions aloud at your interview. [1]
- The officer asks up to **20 questions** from the published list of 128. [1]
- You pass with **12 correct answers**. You fail if you answer 9 incorrectly. [1]
- The officer stops asking once you have answered 12 correctly, or 9 incorrectly. [1]
- You may answer with any of the answers on the official list. USCIS says there may be other
  correct answers, but encourages applicants to use the ones provided. [3]

## Answers that change

Some answers depend on who holds an office (the President, the Vice President, the Speaker of the
House, the Chief Justice, your senators, your representative, your governor). You must give the
name of the official serving **at the time of your interview**. USCIS keeps a page of current
answers. [3] [4]

## The English test

The civics test is one part of the naturalization test. The other is English: [2]

- **Speaking:** the officer judges your ability to speak and understand English during the
  interview about your application.
- **Reading:** you read aloud 1 of 3 sentences correctly.
- **Writing:** you write 1 of 3 sentences correctly.

## If you are 65 or older

If you are 65 or older and have been a lawful permanent resident for 20 years or more: [3]

- you may study only the 20 questions marked with an asterisk on the official list;
- you may take the civics test in the language of your choice;
- the officer asks 10 of those 20, and you pass with 6 correct.

## Disability

The civics requirement does not apply to a person who cannot show the knowledge because of a
medically determinable physical or mental impairment that has lasted, or is expected to last, at
least 12 months. [5]

## Not published in the sources gathered

- **How long the test takes.** No time limit is stated.
- **What to bring.** Follow your appointment notice.
- **Retake rules.** What happens after a fail is not stated on the pages gathered for this pack.
