## Introduction

More than half of the official question list is about how the United States is governed. That makes this the part to know best.

## What the test asks

- **72 of the 128 official questions** are under this heading: 15 on the principles of American government, 47 on the system of government, and 10 on rights and responsibilities. [1]
- Because up to 20 questions are asked, and USCIS does not say how they are chosen, expect roughly eleven of them to come from this part. That is an estimate from the size of the list, not a published rule. [1]

## Key facts

- The Constitution was written in 1787 and is the supreme law of the land. [2, chapter 1]
- There are three branches: Congress makes federal laws, the executive branch enforces them, and the judicial branch reviews them. [2, chapter 1]
- Congress has two parts. The House has 435 voting members elected for two years; the Senate has 100 members elected for six. [2, chapter 2]
- The President is elected for four years, can be elected twice, and is Commander in Chief of the military. [2, chapter 3]
- The Supreme Court has nine justices and is the highest court. [2, chapter 4]
- The First Amendment protects five freedoms: speech, religion, assembly, the press, and petitioning the government. [2, chapter 5]
